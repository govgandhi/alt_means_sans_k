# Physical Review E: referee report (Reviewer 6)

**Manuscript:** "Degree Information and Norm Turnover in Random-Walk Network Embeddings" (G. Gandhi, S. Kojaku, Y.-Y. Ahn).

**Version reviewed:** worktree at commit c958c528 (`paper/current/main.tex`, compiled `main.pdf`, 15 pp.; `supp.tex`/`factor_balance.tex`, `supp.pdf`, 3 pp.), together with the code, protocols, and saved outputs in the repository.

**Reviewer focus:** skip-gram with negative sampling as it is actually trained (Gensim internals, subsampling, negative sampling, learning-rate schedules, initialization) and the evaluation of the embedding experiments.

**How I checked:** every check script is in `<scratch>/round/r6/` (abbreviated `r6/` below). The scripts import the worktree's own graph generator, walk sampler, pair allocator, and trainer unchanged (read-only, no bytecode written) and run in a scratch environment with the pinned stack (Python 3.13, gensim 4.4.0 official wheel, numpy 2.4.1, scipy 1.17.1, networkx 3.6.1). On this independent install the corrected five-epoch unfiltered Gensim control reproduces the saved curves to three decimals (graph 0 raw bins 4.885, 5.192, 5.348, 5.458, 5.605, 5.331; `results/training_trajectory/runs/seed0_t0_baseline_then_floor.json`, epoch 5), so the reruns below are on the authors' exact setup.

## 1. Summary

The paper compares the population geometry of the normalized adjacency eigenspace, the stationary random-walk pair matrix, and the optimal SGNS score under the DCSBM, and shows that unigram ($\alpha=1$) negative sampling cancels node-specific degree from the optimal score. It then reports finite-training experiments on 600-node configuration-model graphs: a custom minibatch SGNS trainer in which positive-center counts are set to $\propto\pi^\beta$ and conditional contexts are mixed toward their marginal by $\lambda$ (Sec. XI.A–C, Fig. 1); Gensim skip-gram runs on short random walks with internal subsampling, repeated after three "corrections" and extended with two learning-rate schedules (Sec. XI.E–F, Figs. 4–5); an exact expected-loss L-BFGS study of windows 5 and 10 (Sec. XI.D); and a hash-frozen ExtraTrees predictor of node norms from realized pair counts and context KL, evaluated on Barabási–Albert, Holme–Kim, and NetScience targets (Sec. XI.G, Fig. 6). The headline experimental claims (abstract, lines 58–64) are that equal, cube-root, and proportional exposure give decreasing, inverted-U, and increasing norm–degree curves; that context mixing removes the decreasing branch; that turnover persists without subsampling under the original schedule but is absent at twenty epochs under a constant rate; and that the frozen predictor transfers to new graphs.

My main finding is that the controlled-intervention results describe a single early stopping point. Re-running the authors' own allocator and trainer with nothing changed except the training budget reverses the $\beta$ ordering in every graph, and at the budget that matches the Gensim runs, proportional exposure produces the same inverted-U as unfiltered Gensim. The learning-rate contrast has the same problem: the constant-rate arm develops turnover later. The Gensim "corrections" are real upstream defects, but their effect on the reported curves is negligible to modest and the paper never says what they are.

## 2. Strengths

1. Provenance is unusually complete: plans and hashes are written before training, historical and corrected outputs are both preserved, and the runners verify sources and runtimes. This is what made an exact independent rerun possible.
2. The authors found and isolated two genuine Gensim 4.x defects (an exception declaration that turns a dot product of exactly $-1$ into $0$, and a negative-sampling table that is permuted when vocabulary keys are Python integers), with toy reproductions and paired control arms (`analysis/negative_sampling_control_protocol.md:7–23, 44–49`).
3. The gauge treatment is careful: Sec. VII separates balanced from raw norms, Proposition 11 explains why nonedge zeros make balanced norms diverge at the loss infimum, and SM Sec. S3 measures native factor imbalance in Gensim (relative Gram imbalance 0.079–0.163, norm discrepancy 0.55–1.15%) instead of assuming it away.
4. Failures are reported: the declared quadratic surface fails at the strongest threshold (:1026), the post hoc degree-and-threshold baseline passes all four prospective criteria (:1171; SM Table S1), and the NetScience misses are stated (:1167–1168).

## 3. Weaknesses, ranked

1. **(Blocker) The exposure and context-mixing results are properties of one training budget.** The controlled trainer receives about 1/11 of the per-node training of the Gensim runs it is meant to explain. With 4× or 12× the budget the qualitative results reverse in 5/5 graphs (Comment 1).
2. **(Major) The learning-rate claim confounds step size with training progress.** The constant-rate arm is behind in loss at epoch 20 and develops interior maxima by epoch 80; annealing is neither necessary nor sufficient; Gensim's own 20-epoch default moves the peak two to three bins (Comment 2).
3. **(Major) The context-mixing intervention, as implemented, changes the negative distribution and context support in a strongly degree-dependent way** (total-variation distance 0.13–0.38 from the intended marginal; low-degree contexts receive almost none of the mixing mass) (Comment 3).
4. **(Major) Training settings and the "corrections" are not reported.** Every Gensim run uses `ns_exponent=1.0` (default 0.75), the controlled trainer's optimizer and initialization are never described, the Gensim version and patched build are not stated, and Gensim is not cited (Comments 4–5).
5. **(Major) The prospective test is conditional on one budget and its frozen run used a permuted negative sampler** that the main text does not disclose (Comment 6).
6. **(Major) The exposure intervention controls only input-vector updates, and at equal exposure per-edge exposure is collinear with context KL** (Comment 7).
7. **(Minor) The readout under-resolves the high-degree tail and the turnover amplitude is gauge-sensitive exactly where it is measured** (Comment 9).
8. **(Minor) Scope beyond Gensim-style per-pair SGD with untied factors and $\alpha=1$ is untested**, and the most common alternative implementation differs on every relevant axis (Comment 11).

## 4. Detailed comments

### Comment 1 (blocker). The controlled intervention results depend on the training budget

- **Location.** Abstract (main.tex:59–60), Introduction (:98–101), Sec. XI.A–C (:964–1022), Discussion (:1185–1186), Conclusion (:1212); Fig. 1c–d (PDF p. 9); `scripts/simulate_turnover_surface.py:77–88` (24 dimensions, 240 positive pairs per node per epoch, 4 epochs, batch 256, rate 0.025→0.0025).
- **Problem.** The $\beta$ and $\lambda$ effects are read off one budget, and that budget is far below the Gensim runs. Per-node progress $\sum_t\eta_t u_i(t)$ is $4\times0.01375\times144{,}000\,\pi_i\approx7.9\times10^3\pi_i$ in the controlled runs at $\beta=1$. In the five-epoch Gensim runs it is $0.06275\times1.44\times10^6\pi_i\approx9.0\times10^4\pi_i$ (1.44 M ordered window-one pairs per epoch from $300n$ five-token walks, mean rate 0.01255). The gap is 11–12×. Line 1139 concedes that "realized positive-pair budgets have not been matched", but no sensitivity check exists, and the paper's own Gensim trajectories (Fig. 5) already show that the curve shape changes with training.
- **Evidence.** `r6/check_controlled_budget.py` (graphs 0–4) varies only `epochs` (4, 16, 48; the trainer stretches its linear decay over the run, as written). `r6/check_controlled_pairs_budget.py` (graphs 0–1) keeps the four-epoch schedule and varies only `events_per_node` (240, 960, 2880). Both call `allocate_pairs` and `train_sgns` from the worktree. Raw-norm shapes on the paper's binning (eight requested log-degree quantiles, six occupied); the balanced-norm classification is identical in every run:

  | Budget relative to paper | $\beta=0$ | $\beta=1/3$ | $\beta=1$ |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1× (4 epochs) | decreasing, 5/5 | inverted-U, 5/5 | increasing, 5/5 |
  | 4× (16 epochs, or 960 pairs/node) | inverted-U, 7/7 (peak bin 4, drop 0.27–0.43) | increasing, 7/7 | increasing, 7/7 |
  | 12× (48 epochs, or 2880 pairs/node; matches Gensim) | increasing, 7/7 | increasing, 7/7 | inverted-U, 7/7 (peak bin 4, drop 0.15–0.33) |

  At the matched budget, proportional exposure gives the unfiltered Gensim shape: peak in bin 4 of 6 with a drop of 0.15–0.33, against 0.19–0.27 in `results/subsampling_origin/README.md:14–18`. Context mixing also changes meaning (`r6/check_controlled_mixing_budget.py`, graph 0, 48 epochs): $\lambda=0$ rises from 5.60 to 6.37, $\lambda=1/3$ is flat (4.52–4.77), and $\lambda=2/3$ falls from 3.60 to 3.28. At this budget mixing creates a decreasing tendency where the unmixed curve increases.
- **Consequence.** "Equal exposure leaves norms decreasing with degree, proportional exposure makes them increase, and exposure proportional to the cube root of frequency produces an inverted-U" and "Mixing node contexts toward their marginal distribution removes the decreasing branch" (:59–60) hold at the chosen stopping point only. The two-force response $\nu(\log m_i,\mathrm{KL}_i)$ of Eq. (68) has no time argument, so it cannot represent this. The data are consistent with a simpler reading that the authors could test: each node's norm trajectory is non-monotone in training time (under constant 0.0025, the top-bin mean is 5.79, 6.12, 6.00, and 5.75 at epochs 5, 20, 40, and 80; `r6/check_lr_schedule.py`), exposure sets how fast a node moves along it, and a fixed-budget norm–degree curve is a snapshot of nodes at different phases. I have not tested this beyond the runs above.
- **Fix.** Re-run the full $\beta\times\lambda$ grid at three or more budgets, including one matched to the Gensim runs in per-node $\sum\eta u$; plot norms against per-node progress as well as degree; rewrite the abstract, introduction, discussion, and conclusion around budget dependence; add progress as an explicit covariate in the response model and the prospective predictor.

### Comment 2 (major). The schedule comparison is a comparison at unequal progress

- **Location.** Abstract :61; :1113–1130; Fig. 5 (p. 12); Discussion :1188–1189; `scripts/training_trajectory/run.py:40–47, 58–64, 86–93`.
- **Problem.** The two arms differ in peak rate (0.025 vs 0.0025), final rate (0.0001 vs 0.0025), cumulative rate at epoch 20 ($\sum\alpha=0.064$ vs 0.050), and optimization progress. After epoch 5 the original arm runs at 0.0001 and gains only 2.4% more cumulative rate, so "retains them through epoch twenty" (:1117) is weak evidence; the protocol says so (`analysis/training_trajectory_protocol.md:28–29`), but the abstract does not. The "original schedule continued to 20 epochs" is also not what Gensim does at `epochs=20`: Gensim decays linearly over all requested epochs (`gensim/models/word2vec.py:1457–1463`).
- **Evidence from the saved runs** (`r6/check_trajectory_matched_progress.py`, unfiltered). The constant arm's expected loss at epoch 20 (0.3801–0.4012) is above the original arm's epoch-5 loss (0.3773–0.3979) in 5/5 graphs. At equal or better loss (constant epoch 10, 0.3857–0.4075, against original epoch 2, 0.4014–0.4225) the constant arm still has no interior maximum, so step size shapes the path beyond loss progress.
- **Evidence from reruns** (`r6/check_lr_schedule.py`, graphs 0–2, unfiltered, same walks, seeds, and slot-ordered table):

  | Schedule | Cumulative $\sum\alpha$ | Interior maximum | Peak bin | Raw drop |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 0.025→0.0001 over 5 epochs (paper) | 0.063 | 3/3 | 4 | 0.21–0.27 |
  | constant 0.0025, epoch 20 (paper) | 0.050 | 0/3 | top | — |
  | constant 0.0025, epoch 40 | 0.100 | 0/3 | top | — |
  | constant 0.0025, epoch 80 | 0.200 | 3/3 | 4 | 0.09–0.14 |
  | constant 0.0025 for 20, then 0.0025→0.0001 over 5 | 0.057 | 0/3 | top | — |
  | paper schedule, then constant 0.0025 for 20 more | 0.113 | 3/3 | 4 | 0.23–0.29 |
  | constant 0.025 for 5 epochs (no annealing) | 0.125 | 3/3 | 2–3 | 0.81–0.84 |
  | Gensim default at `epochs=20` (0.025→0.0001 over 20) | 0.251 | 3/3 | 1–2 | 0.76–0.82 |

- **Consequence.** Annealing is neither necessary (constant 0.025 gives the strongest turnover) nor sufficient (annealing the constant-rate run changes nothing), and the 0.0001 floor does not preserve an otherwise unstable feature (re-heating keeps it). "Absent at twenty epochs under a constant-rate alternative" is literally true and describes slower progress; the same arm develops the turnover later. The supported statement is that the existence, depth, and location of the turnover depend on training progress and step size, and that the peak moves to lower degree as training proceeds.
- **Fix.** Replace the two-arm contrast with a small factorial (initial rate × decay × duration) or present curves against $\sum\alpha$ and against loss; extend checkpoints to at least 80 epochs; include Gensim's own 20-epoch decay; report the matched-loss comparison; rewrite :61, :1118, and :1188–1189.

### Comment 3 (major). Integer allocation undoes the context-mixing intervention

- **Location.** :99, :974–987, :1017–1022, Fig. 1b,d captions (:1007, :1009); `scripts/simulate_turnover_surface.py:204–231`; `scripts/simulate_exact_center_exposure.py:113–126`.
- **Problem.** Eq. (70) preserves $q$ analytically, but each center's 240 pairs are rounded independently by deterministic largest remainder over 600 candidate contexts. The $\lambda q$ component has expected counts of roughly 0.1–0.3 per non-neighbor context, so it is rounded away for low-$q$ (low-degree) contexts in every row and lands on the same mid- and high-degree contexts. Because negatives are drawn from the realized marginal (:986), the noise distribution changes with $\lambda$ in a degree-dependent way. The saved check `maximum_context_marginal_error = 0.0032` (`results/turnover_surface/summary.json`) is a maximum absolute per-node error; with per-node masses near $1/600$ it hides the problem.
- **Evidence** (`r6/check_context_mixing_allocation.py`, `r6/check_mixing_marginal_by_degree.py`, all five graphs). At $\beta\in\{0,1/3\}$ the total-variation distance between the realized and intended context marginal is 0.18–0.20 at $\lambda=1/3$, 0.27–0.31 at $\lambda=2/3$, and 0.31–0.38 at $\lambda=1$ (0.13, 0.19, and 0.22–0.23 at $\beta=1$), against at most 0.003 at $\lambda=0$. At $\beta=0$, contexts of degree about 3 receive 0.67× ($\lambda=1/3$) and 0.34–0.36× ($\lambda=2/3$) of their intended mass, which is $1-\lambda$, i.e., none of the mixing mass; the top bin receives 1.28–1.42×. Mean empirical KL exceeds the intended KL used in Fig. 1b and in the response surface (:1025) by 12–25% (2.69 vs 2.41 at $\lambda=1/3$; 1.15 vs 0.92 at $\lambda=2/3$), its degree slope differs (−0.17 vs −0.24 at $\lambda=2/3$), and their node-level Spearman correlation is 0.84–0.86 at $\lambda=2/3$. At $\beta=0,\lambda=1$ every center receives an identical list of 210–213 contexts and 387–390 contexts never appear as positives or negatives (consistent with `analysis/issue20_realized_graph_audit.md:192–204`). The flat curve at 0.9488 (:1020) is therefore guaranteed by construction: all centers have identical training data.
- **Fix.** Draw pairs i.i.d. from $T^{(\lambda)}$ afresh each epoch, or do one global allocation on the joint $c_iT^{(\lambda)}_{ij}$ that preserves the marginal, or use the exact expected loss as in Sec. XI.D. Report TV distance and empirical KL per $\lambda$, show empirical KL in Fig. 1b, and drop $\lambda=1$ or label it degenerate.

### Comment 4 (major). Training settings that determine the results are not reported

- **Negative-sampling exponent.** Every Gensim run sets `ns_exponent=1.0` (`scripts/simulate_norm_frequency.py:239`, `check_subsampling_origin.py:82`, `training_trajectory/run.py:101`, `prospective_norm_turnover.py:313`, `negative_sampling_control.py:94`). The manuscript never says so; word2vec.c, Gensim, and the node2vec reference code default to 0.75, so a reader of "literal word2vec validation" (:1076) will assume 0.75. The controlled trainer samples negatives from the realized context marginal (`simulate_turnover_surface.py:1101–1105`), which is not of the theory's form $q\propto\delta^\alpha$ when $\beta\neq1$. My check (`r6/check_ns_exponent.py`, graphs 0–2) shows the five-epoch Gensim conclusions are robust: at 0.75 and 0.5 the peak bins are unchanged at $t=0$ and $10^{-3}$, and the unfiltered drop moves from 0.21–0.27 ($\alpha=1$) to 0.21–0.24 (0.75) and 0.18–0.19 (0.5). The exponent still has to be stated, since Table I's predictions depend on it.
- **Controlled trainer.** Lines 989–991 give only dimension, epochs, and negatives. The code uses minibatches of 256 with gradients evaluated at pre-batch parameters and summed through `np.add.at` (`simulate_exact_center_exposure.py:226–272`), a per-step linear rate 0.025→0.0025 (:260–263), centers initialized $U(-0.5/d,0.5/d)$ and contexts at zero (:220–225), collision-allowing negatives (:237–241), the exact sigmoid, and pairs reshuffled each epoch with no walk order. A context with $q\approx0.02$ is drawn about 26 times per batch as a negative, so its output vector receives a 26-term stale summed step, unlike Gensim's sequential updates. Because the trainer reproduces Gensim's unfiltered shape at matched progress (Comment 1), I do not think staleness drives the results, but it must be reported.
- **Gensim.** Version 4.4.0, the rebuilt `word2vec_inner` extension (Cython 3.1.3), `min_count` (50 in the sweeps, 1 in the prospective runs), `shrink_windows=False`, `sorted_vocab=1`, `alpha`/`min_alpha`, and seed offsets are absent from the paper, and Gensim (Řehůřek and Sojka 2010) is not cited.
- **Fix.** One Supplemental table per experiment listing implementation, version, dimension, window, walk length and count, negatives and exponent, negative-count source, collision handling, sigmoid handling, optimizer, batch, schedule, initialization, epochs, positive pairs per epoch, seeds, and workers.

### Comment 5 (major). The "numerical correction" is undefined in the paper, its effect is small, and its relation to prior work is not stated

- **Location.** Abstract :61 ("Corrected word2vec controls"), :1078, :1089, :1108; `README.md:5–32`.
- **What the code changes.** (i) `scripts/build_control_gensim.py` downloads the SHA-256-verified gensim-4.4.0 sdist, edits only the declarations of `sdot_ptr` and `dsdot_ptr` in `word2vec_inner.pxd` from `except -1 nogil` to `noexcept nogil` (:68–82), cythonizes only `gensim.models.word2vec_inner` (:85–108), and replaces the installed `.so` and `.pxd` inside a virtual environment (:131–150). No sampling or update logic changes. (ii) `scripts/gensim_training.py:31–57` overwrites `model.cum_table` after `build_vocab` with slot-ordered counts raised to `ns_exponent`, rounded to $2^{31}-1$. (iii) `sample_walks` now counts every visit with `np.add.at` (`simulate_norm_frequency.py:165`); this repairs a project bug (tracked as #44).
- **Are (i) and (ii) upstream bugs?** Both are. (i) In 4.4.0 `our_dot_float` is `noexcept` while `sdot` is declared `except -1` (`word2vec_inner.pxd:25–26`); the generated C treats a return of exactly $-1.0$f as an error, prints "Exception ignored", and returns 0 (`word2vec_inner.c`, error label sets `__pyx_r = 0`). With the official wheel, `r6/check_gensim_upstream.py` gets $0.0$ for $1\times(-1)$. Gensim 4.3.3 declared both pointers plain `nogil`; upstream issue #3665 was open when checked on 2026-11-12, with PR #3668 linked. (ii) Gensim 4.x `make_cum_table` passes slot positions to `get_vecattr` (`word2vec.py:838–845`), and `get_index` resolves an integer as a key before treating it as a position (`keyedvectors.py:407–420`), so with integer node IDs slot $p$ receives the count of node $p$. Gensim 3.8.3 used `wv.vocab[wv.index2word[i]].count` and was correct. I found no upstream report of (ii).
- **Magnitudes the paper should state.** (i) Five affected dot products across 45 unpatched runs, maximum node-norm change 0.065, no peak-bin change (`results/negative_sampling_control/comparison_report.json`; `archive/negative_sampling_control/gensim_4_4_0_unpatched/training_log.txt`). (ii) Total-variation distance between the legacy and intended negative distributions is 0.13–0.15 on the five calibration graphs, whose node IDs track degree (Spearman 0.69–0.73 with frequency rank), 0.14 on a direct BA target, and 0.38 on NetScience, whose IDs are unrelated to degree (per-node probabilities off by factors from 0.08 to 15.7; `r6/check_gensim_upstream.py`). Between the legacy and correct arms the node-norm RMSE is 0.106, Spearman 0.974, with no peak-bin change (`results/negative_sampling_control/summary.json`).
- **Prior work.** String-token pipelines and Gensim ≤4.3.3 (or word2vec.c) are unaffected by (i) and (ii), and the cited inverted-U studies predate 4.4.0. The paper should say so, because "corrected word2vec" otherwise suggests that earlier inverted-U findings are suspect. The paper's runs do differ from standard practice in other ways: $\alpha=1$, window 1, five-token walks, one worker.
- **Fix.** Add an implementation-notes paragraph to Sec. XI and an SM table (defect, affected versions and inputs, where fixed, measured effect); report (ii) upstream.

### Comment 6 (major). The prospective transfer is budget-conditional, and the frozen run's sampler is undisclosed

- **Location.** :1153–1173; Fig. 6 (p. 13); SM S1.
- **Frozen sampler.** The frozen predictions and outcomes reported in the main text come from the pipeline with the integer-key table (TV 0.38 on NetScience; Comment 5) and retained-count noise (issue #17, tracked). The revalidation shows that all four criteria still pass (`results/negative_sampling_revalidation/README.md:11–18`). That reassurance belongs in the main text next to the numbers.
- **Frozen retention.** External subsampling draws one retained corpus and reuses it for all five epochs (`prospective_norm_turnover.py:219–235`, then `sample=0.0` at :317). Gensim redraws retention for every token in every epoch (`word2vec_inner.pyx:544`), so the external variant sees one fifth of the distinct positive-pair realizations. :1195 calls the procedure "external" but does not mention this.
- **Budget.** Calibration and targets share one budget. In literal Gensim at a fixed threshold, moving from the five-epoch decay to Gensim's 20-epoch decay shifts the raw-norm peak from bins 4, 3, 3 to 1, 3, 1 at $t=10^{-3}$ and from 4, 3, 4 to 1, 3, 1 at $t=3\times10^{-3}$, with drops growing 1.8–4.8× (`r6/check_budget_vs_threshold.py`, graphs 0–2). At 20 epochs the $10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-3}$ peaks coincide in 3/3 graphs. The budget moves the peak by as many bins as the threshold does in Fig. 4, so the "peak movement with weaker subsampling" (:102, :1166) is budget-specific.
- **Fix.** State the sampler history and the single retention draw in the main text; restrict the transfer claim to the fixed budget or add a cross-budget test; add training progress as a predictor input.

### Comment 7 (major). The exposure intervention is one-sided and confounds per-edge exposure with specificity

- **Location.** :98, :964–973, :996–1000; `simulate_turnover_surface.py:196–231`.
- **Evidence** (`r6/check_exposure_sides.py`, graph 0, per epoch). Input-side positive updates are exactly 240 in every degree bin at $\beta=0$. Output-side updates are untouched by $\beta$: positive-context updates rise from about 120 (degree ≈3) to about 725 (top bin), and expected negative updates from about 600 to about 3,600, at all three values of $\beta$. Pairs are i.i.d. and shuffled, so there are no window positions or walk order to hold fixed. Exact allocation makes each center's empirical context distribution equal $T_i$, so context diversity per center is fixed at $d_i$ distinct contexts. Per-edge exposure $m_i/d_i$, however, is $240/d_i$ at $\beta=0$, decreasing in $d_i$ as $\mathrm{KL}_i\approx\log(\mathrm{vol}/d_i)-\langle\log d_j\rangle$ does. In this design exposure per context and specificity are collinear, and Comment 1 shows the $\beta=0$ decreasing branch disappears once per-edge exposure grows.
- **Fix.** State that only input-vector updates are controlled. Add a design that separates per-edge exposure from KL at fixed budget (for example, weighted or multi-step targets with matched KL but different numbers of distinct contexts), or show saturation in budget.

### Comment 8 (minor). Subsampling: formula, application, and measured exposure law

- **Formula.** Eq. (65) is the word2vec.c/Gensim form, keep $=\sqrt{t/p}+t/p$ (`gensim/models/word2vec.py:734`). Mikolov et al. (2013) publish the discard probability $1-\sqrt{t/f}$, i.e., keep $=\sqrt{t/p}$, so the citation at :911 should point to the implementation. The cutoff $2.62t$ and Eq. (67) are correct.
- **Application.** Gensim deletes tokens in both roles before windowing and redraws every epoch (`word2vec_inner.pyx:540–546`: "shortening the sentence = expanding the window"). The controlled held-out "retention law" conditions (Fig. 1a, :1024–1028) apply the law to center counts only, with contexts from unmodified one-step $T_i$ and negatives from that marginal (`simulate_turnover_surface.py:154–165`). They do not emulate Gensim's subsampling and should be labeled center-only.
- **Measured scaling** (`r6/check_subsampling_exposure.py`, five calibration corpora, Gensim retention applied per token). At $t=3\times10^{-4}$, 591–596 of 600 nodes are subsampled and the bin-to-bin log–log slope of retained tokens on frequency is 0.33–0.39 over the whole degree range (Eq. 67 gives 0.41 for the top 5% of nodes). At $10^{-3}$ the slope is 1.0 in five of six bins and about 0.5 only in the top bin; at $3\times10^{-3}$ only 8–12 nodes are subsampled. The largest $p/t$ is 56–70, so the square-root asymptote invoked at :1187 is never reached; the realized law at the strongest threshold is close to the $\beta=1/3$ intervention. Realized window-one pairs per retained token fall from 1.60 to 1.30–1.33 at $t=3\times10^{-4}$, almost uniformly in degree, and 28–31% of the retained pairs (9–11% at $10^{-3}$, 1–3% at $3\times10^{-3}$) join tokens that were not adjacent in the raw walk. Strong subsampling thus turns about a third of the positive pairs into lag-two-or-more pairs, a context-mixing effect that :929 mentions without quantifying.
- **Fix.** Report the measured elasticities and the non-adjacent share; restate :1187 with the measured range; relabel the held-out controlled conditions.

### Comment 9 (minor). Tail resolution and gauge sensitivity of the turnover readout

- **Location.** Captions of Figs. 1, 4, 5 (:1012, :1095, :1129); :1110; `simulate_gensim_threshold_sweep.py:70–94`.
- **Evidence** (`r6/check_tail_resolution.py` on `results/subsampling_origin/outcomes.csv`). With eight requested log-degree quantiles the top occupied bin spans degrees 10–75 (345 of 3,000 node rows, 11.5%), so the entire decreasing branch is one plotted point. In fixed bins the unfiltered raw norm peaks at degree 9–11 (5.59) and falls monotonically through 5.45 (12–16), 5.22 (17–25), and 4.78 (26–40) to 4.39 (≥41, 20 node rows), a 21% decline; the reported drop of 0.244 and the peak "near degree 7.73" (:1110) reflect bin placement. In the balanced gauge the ≥41 bin is 4.72 (a 16% decline), so the turnover amplitude is 25–30% gauge-dependent exactly where it is measured; the largest node discrepancy in SM Table S3 (12.45%) belongs to the degree-73 hub of graph 0 (raw 4.10, balanced 4.61). Degree and realized walk frequency do coincide (Spearman 0.96–0.97; tokens within 0.89–1.16 of the stationary expectation; `r6/check_degree_vs_frequency.py`), so binning on degree is fine.
- **Fix.** Use fixed log-spaced bins with at least three bins above the peak, or a smoother with bootstrap bands over graphs; report the amplitude in both gauges. The paper's own prescription (:682) argues for making the balanced norm primary.

### Comment 10 (minor). Which factor is measured, and the unused output vectors

Raw norms are input vectors throughout: `model.wv.vectors` reindexed by `key_to_index` (`simulate_norm_frequency.py:254–256`) and `center_vectors` in the controlled trainer (`simulate_turnover_surface.py:1108`). In Gensim's skip-gram the input vector of the context-position token predicts the output vector of the center-position token (`word2vec_inner.pyx:574–591`); with symmetric windows the pair multiset is symmetric, so this matches the paper's "center" factor and the theory's $U$. The balanced readout (QR of both factors plus SVD of $R_UR_V^\top$; `simulate_norm_frequency.py:170–184`) implements Eq. (balanced-factors) correctly. Gensim 4.4.0 initializes input vectors $U(-1/d,1/d)$ (mean norm 0.072 at $d=64$; `keyedvectors.py:2116–2120`) and output vectors at zero, so the initialization residual is negligible against final norms near 5 and the asymmetric start does not distort either readout; it does mean the conserved-imbalance argument (:684–688) gives no guarantee, as SM S3 states. Table I makes separate predictions for context rows when $\alpha\neq1$, yet no experiment reports `syn1neg` norms, which are already computed. The controlled trainer's $U(-0.5/d,0.5/d)$ is called "the standard Word2Vec initialization" (`simulate_exact_center_exposure.py:217`); that is the word2vec.c and Gensim 3 scale, half the range of the Gensim 4.4.0 initialization used in every other experiment.

### Comment 11 (minor). Implementation dependence and scope

Gensim skips the whole update when the score is outside $(-6,6)$ (`word2vec_inner.pyx:226–227`). I rebuilt the official 4.4.0 source with the exact sigmoid in that range instead of the skip (`r6/build_noskip/`, `r6/check_noskip.py`): the five-epoch peak stays in bin 4 in 3/3 graphs with a 18–23% smaller drop (0.16–0.23), and the 20-epoch decay still peaks in bins 1–3. The skip rule therefore deepens the turnover without creating it. Other implementations differ much more. PyTorch Geometric's `Node2Vec` uses one embedding table for both roles (tied factors), draws negatives uniformly over node IDs ($\alpha=0$), keeps PyTorch's default $N(0,1)$ embedding initialization (norm about $\sqrt d$), and its documented example trains with SparseAdam at rate 0.01. The paper's own symmetric-score result (main.tex:613–637) and Corollary 9 predict a different population target in that setting. Given Comments 1–2, the experimental conclusions currently apply to Gensim-style per-pair SGD with untied factors, $\alpha=1$, and a five-epoch budget, and the abstract and discussion should say so; one untied PyTorch SGNS run (plain SGD and Adam, matched budgets) would show whether they extend.

### Comment 12 (minor). Smaller items

- :1114 describes 0.0025 as "the final rate used by the separate controlled trainer". That trainer decays linearly from 0.025 to 0.0025 over its run, so the constant 0.0025 arm corresponds to no schedule used elsewhere and needs its own rationale.
- :1100–1103: matching retained-token budgets by changing epochs (8, 6, 5) also changes every node's training progress, which by Comment 2 moves the peak. The design cannot isolate exposure even after correction.
- :891 and :1136–1137 note Gensim's collision skip and update window; the controlled trainer's collision-allowing negatives and exact sigmoid should be stated alongside.
- :1079: five-token walks with window one are far from DeepWalk and node2vec practice (walk length 40–80, window 5–10). The only multi-window evidence uses an expected-loss L-BFGS fit (Sec. XI.D). A literal Gensim run at window 5 would show whether the shape survives the estimator that practitioners use.

## 7. Questions for the authors

1. Was the controlled budget (240 pairs per node per epoch, four epochs) fixed before the $\beta$ pattern was seen, and on what basis? How do Fig. 1c–d look at 16 and 48 epochs, and at a budget matched in per-node $\sum\eta u$ to the Gensim runs?
2. Why `ns_exponent=1.0` for the "literal" runs, and what exponent, window, walk length, and Gensim version did the cited inverted-U studies use?
3. Do the authors interpret the turnover as a property of a training snapshot (non-monotone per-node norm trajectories traversed at exposure-dependent speed)? If so, how should the population results of Secs. III–VII be connected to it?
4. Would the frozen ExtraTrees predictor, calibrated at five epochs, predict the 20-epoch Gensim peaks? Was the training budget part of the frozen protocol's scope statement?
5. Why deterministic per-row largest-remainder allocation for $\lambda>0$, and how do Fig. 1b,d change with i.i.d. per-epoch resampling?
6. What do output-vector (`syn1neg`) norms show, given Table I's distinct prediction for context rows?
7. Has the integer-key `make_cum_table` behavior been reported upstream?
8. Does the turnover survive in literal Gensim at window 5 with `shrink_windows=True`, and in an untied PyTorch SGNS with SGD and with Adam?

## 8. Recommendation

**Major revision.** **Confidence: 4/5.**

The population results (Secs. III–VII) look correct and are carefully scoped, though much of the content restates established results, as the authors acknowledge. My concerns are with the experimental half, which carries the abstract's novel claims. Re-running the authors' own trainer with only the budget changed reverses the exposure and mixing conclusions in every graph, and the schedule conclusion is a comparison at unequal progress whose constant-rate arm develops turnover later. Both findings come from the authors' unchanged code on an install that reproduces their saved outputs exactly, so I am confident in them. The repairs are feasible because every run takes seconds to minutes, but they change what the experimental sections can claim: the paper would need to present the norm–degree curve as a function of training progress, re-implement the mixing intervention, and scope the transfer result. My confidence is not 5 because I did not audit the population proofs or the L-BFGS window study in depth.
